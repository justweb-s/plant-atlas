Plant Atlas

A plant safety officer asks: can this machine be used today, and what do I have to do, by when? The answer is spread over the law, the machine manual and the plant's own equipment list. We turn those documents into an ontology in which every fact carries the exact words of its source, or it does not get in.

The problem, on a real list

IGEA S.p.A., a company that works in mining areas in Sardinia, publishes the list of its 24 lifting machines verified: IGEA technical sheet, public PDF. The sheet says each one must be verified at the intervals of Annex VII of Legislative Decree 81/2008. Annex VII has 29 rows. Which row applies depends on whether the machine is fixed or mobile, on the sector, and on whether it is more than ten years old.

So the same overhead crane, built in 2017, is verified every two years today and every year from 2028. No law changes in between: the crane gets older. A search over the documents finds the two rows. It does not tell the safety officer which one is his.

What you will see in the demo

  1. Ask. «Who carries out the first periodic verification, and within what time?» The answer is INAIL, within 45 days, and under it the sentence of Article 71 it comes from, word for word.
  2. Two sources disagree. The 2011 ministerial decree says sixty days; Article 71 in force says forty-five. The higher source wins, and the old fact stays visible, closed.
  3. The cell. Four real machines (two Universal Robots arms, a SICK safety scanner, a MAHA vehicle lift) with the facts taken from their official manuals: what must be checked every six months, every year, and the warning that a robot arm alone cannot be put into service.
  4. Why trust it. The scoreboard, including the questions we get wrong.

The app runs on our laptops (Next.js and a small Python server); there is no public deployment. Repository: github.com/justweb-s/plant-atlas.

The rule: no quote, no fact

A model proposes a fact and points to the sentence it comes from. Code then checks, word by word, that the sentence is in the text, that the value is in the sentence, and that an «if» or «unless» in the text has not been lost.

Why it is hard to copy

The questions and their expected answers come before the system and are public, so any other approach can be scored on the same table. The check that rejects a fact is code, not a prompt: it cannot be talked out of it. And when a source changes, the old fact is closed with its date instead of being overwritten, so an answer can be given as of any day.

Models and AI tools

  • In the product: anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5 to propose facts, anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5 to answer, openai/text-embedding-3-small for plain search, all through the Vercel AI Gateway. Every response is cached in the repository, so the numbers can be reproduced without paying again.
  • To build it: Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2) and the 8hack skill. The full list is in AI_TOOLS.md.
  • How outputs were checked: every quote in the expected answers is searched in the official text by a test; every fact in the ontology passes the same check before it is stored.
  • What the model got wrong, and how it was caught: 66 of the facts it proposed had a quote that is not in the text, a value that is not in the quote, or a lost condition. The check rejected and counted them.
