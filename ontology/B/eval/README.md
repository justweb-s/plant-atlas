# Reference sample of extracted facts (strategy B)

1. **What it is.** `facts_sample.json` holds 88 facts of the B store with status "current", each read against its source text and judged true to the text or not.
2. **How it was drawn.** Seed 20261114, Python `random.Random(seed)` over the current facts sorted by id. First 6 model-written facts per relation (appliesFrom; repealedFrom and placingAllowedUntil together, all 3; retentionPeriod; hasTimeLimit; verifiedBy; requiresProcedure; listedIn; mustAccompany; definedAs; hasObligation; addressesRisk; requiresMeasure), then top-ups until the manufacturer, guidance, company and plant layers have at least 5 each, then a round-robin over those four layers up to 80.
3. **Code-written facts.** Facts whose `asserted_by` starts with "code:" are excluded from those 80; 8 of them are drawn separately and flagged `"by_code": true`.
4. **How it was judged.** A coding assistant read the whole piece each fact points to (a window of about 4,000 characters around the quote when the piece is longer) and the neighbouring pieces when the sentence refers to them; corrigendum facts were read against the article of the Regulation they correct. No model call, and none of the product's own checkers, reports or learned rules were read.
5. **Fields.** `relation_fits`, `value_right`, `subject_right`, `condition_judged` (complete, missing, wrong, none_needed; `condition` is the fact's own text), `verdict` (correct, minor, wrong), `why` (one sentence quoting the deciding words), `error_type` (only when not correct). `value_as_written` counted when judging floors such as "at least".
6. **Result.** 47 correct, 31 minor, 10 wrong; the 8 code-written facts are all correct.
7. **Limits.** One judge, 88 facts out of 2,073, no legal expert: the fact is judged against the passage, not against the whole law. The line between "minor" and "wrong" is a judgement; the bare counts are too small to compare relations with each other.
8. **Known soft spots.** A missing starting point ("from the request") is "minor", a missing direction ("at least 45 days before the deadline") is "wrong"; an option recorded as the required procedure is "minor" when the sibling options exist as facts.
9. **How to use it.** Re-run the extraction, look up these ids (or the same source piece and relation when ids change) and judge them again the same way.
10. **What counts as better.** A change to the extraction is better if, on these ids, it turns "wrong" or "minor" facts into correct ones without touching the "correct" ones; a change that fixes some and breaks others is not.
